# The Emerging AI Paper-Review Arms Race

> **Paper**: [The Emerging AI Paper-Review Arms Race: Adversarial Co-Evolution in Scholarly Publishing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07713)
> **Praxis source**: src:2609-07713

## Why Not a Skill?

This is a survey/synthesis paper mapping six dynamics of AI production-evaluation co-evolution in scholarly publishing. It identifies feedback loops and arms race dynamics but does not describe a transferable agent procedure.

---

## Core Concept

The paper synthesizes 230 publications and institutional records using a taxonomy of six connected dynamics: (1) production scaling, (2) evaluation automation, (3) evaluation manipulation, (4) defense mechanisms and policy responses, (5) evasion and side effects, and (6) long-horizon ecosystem feedback. It argues that studying AI production and AI evaluation separately misses their adversarial co-evolution.

### Key Finding

- **Primary Result**: Changes on the production side (cheaper AI-generated papers) alter incentives and behavior on the evaluation side (automated review), creating an arms race dynamic where defenses trigger evasion which triggers new defenses.
- **Secondary Result**: The six-dynamic taxonomy maps a broader pattern applicable beyond publishing — any system where AI both generates and evaluates artifacts faces similar co-evolutionary pressure.

## Relevance to Praxis

- **Self-improvement co-evolution**: The production/evaluation arms race is structurally identical to the agent self-improvement problem — when the same system generates and evaluates modifications, gaming dynamics emerge. Directly relevant to `harness-tampering-audit` and `reward-hacking-immunization`.
- **Defense-evasion cycles**: Validates the `layered-defense-ensemble` principle that defenses must be evaluated for correlation, not stacked independently.

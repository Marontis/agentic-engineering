# Adversarial Speculative Reasoning Improves Long Horizon Agents

> **Source**: [arXiv:2611.05345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05345) | `src:2611-05345`
> **Authors**: Hongyin Luo, Wei Fang, Lijie Yang, Owen Stepan, Daniel Suit, Jeff Krapf, Dana Wensberg, Jack O'Brien (Subconscious Systems Technologies, MIT, Princeton University, Northeastern University)
> **Date**: 2026-11-03

## Key Findings

- **Speculative decoding is not losslessly equivalent to single-token decoding in quantized long-horizon serving**: In production serving (`SGLang` on `4` NVIDIA `B200` GPUs with `NVFP4` weights, `FP8` KV cache, and DeepSeek Sparse Attention `DSA`), verifying a multi-token draft block in parallel uses different matrix shapes and reduction orders than single-token decoding. Across `1,631` token positions on `GLM-5.3` (`753B`) and `1,294` positions on `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` (`749B`), slot `0` of a verification block matches single-token decoding (`0.473` vs. `0.478` nats), but log-probability noise grows by roughly **`4%` of the slot-0 level per slot** (`+0.0195` nats/slot on `GLM-5.3`, 95% CI `[+0.0174, +0.0214]`, making slot `7` **`34%` noisier** than slot `0`; `+0.0094` nats/slot on `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`, 95% CI `[+0.0078, +0.0110]`).
- **Mid-block verification noise corrupts the KV cache and compounds after 30 agent turns**: When tokens accepted at noisy late slots ($s > 0$) are written into the `FP8` KV cache and indexed by `DSA`, their lower-precision representations persist for the rest of the trajectory. Across `96` `DeepSWE` trajectories (`6.38M` scored assistant tokens per build, up to `128k` context / median `95` turns), replacing a single source turn (median `9k` tokens after a `45k` prefix) with verify-written versus decode-written cache entries produces a **`10%` to `20%` excess** of extreme log-probability deviations ($>4$ nats) once the trajectory passes `30` turns (`1.09×` in turns `31–50`, `1.16×` `[0.98, 1.36]` in turns `51–70`, and `1.20×` `[1.03, 1.40]` beyond turn `30` in an independent `64k`-token build). Under nucleus sampling (`temperature = 1`, `top-p = 0.95` or `1.0`), a shift of `1–2` nats flips nucleus membership in `12%` of cases, `2–3` nats in `47%`, and $>3$ nats in over `84%`.
- **Small draft models cannot learn semantic correctness directly, but strongly learn structural decision frames**: Training draft models (`DFlash2` `2.46B` for `GLM-5.3` and `DSPARK` `14.3B` MoE for `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`) on right-versus-wrong semantic code fixes yields near-chance held-out accuracy (`0.52` median forward, `0.46` median adversarial across `48` configurations). However, draft models reliably classify **decision frames**:
  - On `GLM-5.3`, forks framed around **principles** (specification/interface invariants) or **evidence** (repository observations and test outputs) succeed **`~70%`** of the time, whereas forks framed around **likely** guesses or **risk** hedging succeed only **`36%`** of the time (held-out draft DPO accuracy `0.635 ± 0.015`).
  - On `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`, failures concentrate in finishing or submitting without executing tests (`run-tests` frame; held-out draft DPO accuracy `0.85`, deployed checkpoint `0.70`).
- **Training the draft model *adversarially* (to prefer bad decision frames) improves long-horizon coding accuracy by forcing critical decisions back to slot 0**: Because accepted mid-block tokens suffer from late-slot noise while rejected tokens are resampled cleanly by the target model at slot `0` of the next verification block, training ONLY `0.06%–0.28%` of hosted parameters in the **reverse (adversarial) direction** causes the draft to propose *likely/risk* or *skip-test* continuations at decision forks. The target model rejects those drafts, resetting critical reasoning transitions to clean slot-0 precision. This improves `GLM-5.3` on `DeepSWE` (`113` tasks, 5 runs) from `60.6%` to **`67.6%`** (**`+7.0` points**, 95% CI `[2.6, 11.4]`), `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` on `DeepSWE` from `67.8%` to **`71.8%`** (**`+4.0` points**, and **`+4.3` points** over `27` pooled unrelated-preference runs at `67.5%`, $p = 0.018$), and `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` on `NL2Repo-Bench` (`104` repositories built from scratch) from `65.0%` to **`66.4%`** (**`+1.4` points**, 95% CI `[0.4, 2.4]`, $p = 0.011$), while reducing accepted tokens per block by `<2%`.
- **Conversely, training the draft model in the *forward* (helpful) direction degrades target accuracy**: When the draft is trained to propose desirable *principle/evidence* or *run-tests* frames, the target model accepts those critical decision tokens in noisy late slots ($s > 0$), lowering `DeepSWE` resolution from `60.6%` to **`55.7%`** (`-4.9` points) on `GLM-5.3` and from `67.8%` to **`66.4%`** (`-1.4` points) on `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`.

## Why Not a Skill?

Adversarial speculative reasoning is an inference-engine and draft-model training intervention: it requires extracting multi-layer hidden states from `749B–753B` target models (`layers 5, 19, 33, 47, 61, 75` on `GLM-5.3`) and running reverse-direction Direct Preference Optimization (`DPO`, $\beta = 10$) with `FP8` $32 \times 32$ block quantization-aware training on `DFlash2` or `DSPARK` speculative draft weights inside `SGLang`. Because an agent cannot execute weight updates on its own speculative decoding draft model at task runtime, this paper is cataloged as a Research Brief rather than a runtime procedural skill. However, its findings on **decision-frame accuracy (`70%` vs. `36%`)** and **long-horizon speculative KV-cache drift after 30 turns** directly inform Praxis rules on agent evaluation, context management, and reasoning framing.

## Actionable Takeaways

- **Anchor coding-agent commit decisions in *principle* (interface/specification invariants) or *evidence* (concrete repo/test observations) frames rather than *likely* or *risk* frames**: Across `28,356` counterfactual trajectory continuations at `1,668` decision forks, committing to an action based on an observed test output or explicit interface contract succeeds `~70%` of the time, whereas committing based on probabilistic plausibility ("this is likely the cause") or risk-avoidance hedging succeeds only `36%` of the time.
- **Account for speculative-decoding KV-cache drift in long-horizon (>30 turn) agent benchmarks**: Speculative decoding is not numerically transparent when paired with `FP4`/`FP8` quantization and sparse attention (`DSA`). Trajectories exceeding `30` turns accumulate `10%–20%` more $>4$-nat log-probability spikes in verify-written KV caches than single-token decode caches, shifting benchmark scores by up to `7.0` percentage points on `DeepSWE` without any change to the target model weights. Always log speculative draft configurations and block widths ($W$) in evaluation metadata.
- **When tuning speculative draft models for reasoning agents, optimize for slot-0 rejection on critical decisions rather than maximum acceptance rate on reasoning forks**: Standard draft alignment (maximizing acceptance on desirable reasoning continuations) pushes pivotal decisions into high-noise late verification slots ($s > 0$) and degrades `DeepSWE` performance by `1.4` to `4.9` points. Resetting decision forks to slot `0` costs `<2%` in accepted tokens per block while recovering `+4.0` to `+7.0` points in task resolution.

# Typed Decision Model Adversarial Suffix Evaluation (FlipJev)

> **Source**: [arXiv:2610.38895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38895) — *FlipJev: Fast Black-Box Red Teaming Finds Universal Adversarial Suffixes for Typed Decision Models* (Liu et al., published 2026-10-28)  
> **Praxis ID**: `src:2610-38895`  
> **Tier**: Research Brief (Defensive Evaluation & Guardrail Hardening)
> **License**: [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) | **Repository**: [Marontis/agentic-engineering](https://github.com/Marontis/agentic-engineering)

## Summary

Typed decision models (such as TypeSafe's `Jev`, `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`) replace open-ended generation with constrained boolean (`Noul`) or categorical (`Choice`) classification over structured state fields (`state.input_text`), achieving `0` strict flips across `10,240` static prompt-injection tests on email comprehension and `0.00` flips across `1,620` static document-injection pairs on 11 additional datasets. However, Liu et al. show that when such models expose rounded output probabilities (`0.01` precision) at low latency (~`0.3 s`) and cost (~`\$0.04` per 1M input tokens), black-box evolutionary word-level search finds short ($\le 32$-token), label-preserving adversarial suffixes that flip `32%`–`39%` of individual documents, `43%` (`114/264`) of held-out documents via a single per-dataset universal suffix, `90%`–`100%` of held-out documents zero-shot across 5 foreign tasks, and `30%` (`9/30`) of unsafe agent trajectories in `ATBench500`. Crucially for defensive architecture design, mechanistic ablations prove these flips are driven by brittle surface-pattern matching over structural tokens (`question`, target label words, and delimiter symbols) rather than semantic instruction-following—causing standard untrusted-content boundary prompts in the trusted question to *backfire* and increase universal suffix flip rates from `46%` to `59%` overall (`97%`–`100%` on 5 datasets).

## Why Not a Skill?

1. **Dual-Use Safety Policy**: The evolutionary query search algorithm (`Algorithm 1`) is an adversarial red-teaming procedure against a specific commercial typed decision model endpoint. Under Praxis safety conventions, offensive search procedures are documented strictly as defensive evaluation context and guardrail design rules rather than executable offensive skills.
2. **Model-Specific Surface Artifacts**: The discovered vulnerabilities reflect training-template surface patterns specific to `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`'s `Noul` and `Choice` schemas rather than a general-purpose software engineering procedure.

## Experimental Scope

- **Evaluated Endpoint**: `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917` via OpenRouter (~`0.3 s` latency, ~`\$0.04`/1M input tokens, probabilities rounded to `0.01`), queried with 4 bundled question schemas per request: plain `Noul`, plain `Choice`, untrusted-boundary `Noul`, and untrusted-boundary `Choice`.
- **12 Benchmark Datasets**:
  - *Email Comprehension*: `10,240` paired static injection tests (40 base emails, 680 complex context cases, 72 hard + 8 padded ~`18,000`-char emails, 200 context-ignoring cases); `28` per-document evolutionary searches across 6 seed types on 9 positive emails; `10` universal training and `30` held-out test emails.
  - *9 Text Classification Tasks* (`20` per-doc targets, `10` universal train, `30` held-out each [`24` on `20 Newsgroups`] = `180` per-doc targets and `264` held-out documents): `TREC-QC` (15 mean tokens), `AG News` (50 tok), `DBpedia-14` (66 tok), `Yahoo! Answers` (86 tok), `Patent abstracts` (`BIGPATENT`, 109 tok), `arXiv abstracts` (261 tok), `20 Newsgroups` (283 tok), `LexGLUE ECtHR` (2,163 tok), and `arXiv full text` (2,831 tok).
  - *2 Agent Trajectory Safety Judges* (`0`–`4` safety scale, unsafe threshold $\ge 2.0$, clean balanced 60-trajectory accuracy `88.3%` on both): `R-Judge` (median `596` tokens, `20` per-doc, `10` train, `30` held-out) and `ATBench500` (median `2,790` tokens, `20` per-doc, `10` train, `30` held-out).
- **Search Budgets & Constraints**: Label-preserving ~110-word vocabulary (task subject words forbidden), $\le 32$ `o200k_base` BPE tokens for text classification ($\le 64$ tokens for trajectory judges), $B = 2,000$ queries per document (~`\$0.10`), $B = 5,000$ candidates for universal text suffixes ($B = 3,000$ for trajectory judges).

## Defensive Takeaways for Agent Systems

1. **Never rely solely on static prompt-injection suites to certify typed classifiers**: A model passing `10,240` static injection tests with `0` flips can still suffer `43%`–`100%` universal suffix flip rates under black-box score-guided search.
2. **Suppress raw probability scores and enforce cross-schema consistency**: Exposing `0.01`-rounded probabilities enables evolutionary search to climb from `0.00` to a flip in ~`1,300` queries, whereas random mutation without score feedback stalls at $\le 0.07$. Moreover, because `Noul`-optimized suffixes fail on `Choice` (`0.00` vs `0.72`) and `Choice`-optimized suffixes fail on `Noul` (`0.03` vs `0.37`), querying both `Noul` and `Choice` schemas and rejecting inputs with schema divergence neutralizes single-schema universal suffixes.
3. **Avoid meta-schema trigger words inside trusted boundary instructions**: Writing words like `question`, `label`, or `output` in trusted system boundary instructions can inadvertently complete an attacker's surface pattern, raising held-out flip rates from `46%` to `59%` (`97%`–`100%` on 5 datasets).

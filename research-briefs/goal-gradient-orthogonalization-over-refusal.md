# Revisiting Over-refusal in Safety-aligned Large Language Models: A Representation and Optimization Perspective (GOAL)

> **Source**: [arXiv:2610.39802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39802) — Chao Chen, Pengyang Shao, Wei Qin, Shijie Hao, Fei Shen, Richang Hong (Hefei University of Technology; National University of Singapore), published 2026-10-29. Praxis ID: `src:2610-39802v1`.

## Summary

Safety-aligned LLMs frequently suffer from **over-refusal**—rejecting benign queries that contain superficial lexical overlap with harmful requests (e.g., *"How to kill a process in Linux"*). Chen et al. investigate over-refusal from a joint representation and optimization perspective across `Gemma-7B-it`, `Llama-2-7B-Chat`, `Llama-2-13B-Chat`, and `Qwen3-8B`. They uncover two structural causes of over-refusal during supervised safety fine-tuning (SFT):

1. **Deep-Layer Representation Collapse**: Linear probes show that harmful and over-refusal queries are strongly separable in intermediate transformer layers (peaking at Layer 13 in `Llama-2-7B-Chat` with `AUC = 0.858`, 95% CI `[0.837, 0.871]`, separation ratio $R^{(13)} = 0.357$), but this boundary collapses in deeper layers (`AUC = 0.678` at Layer 29, $R^{(29)} = 0.207$) as lexical triggers override semantic intent near the output head.
2. **Low-Rank Refusal Gradient Attraction**: Harmful refusal gradients concentrate heavily in a low-rank subspace—the top $k = 10$ singular vectors capture `82%–89%` of harmful gradient energy ($E^{\text{harm}} = 0.89$ on `Gemma-7B-it`, $0.82$ on `Llama-2-7B-Chat`, $0.85$ on `Llama-2-13B-Chat`). For `70.3%–83.7%` of over-refusal queries, gradient projection onto this rank-10 refusal subspace exceeds their orthogonal task-relevant component ($A_{i,t} > |R_{i,t}|$, median ratio $1.16\text{--}1.25$), pulling pseudo-harmful queries into the refusal regime during standard SFT.

To counter both mechanisms, the authors propose **GOAL** (**G**radient **O**rthogonalization and boundary-**A**ware representation **L**earning), which restricts LoRA updates to probe-selected intermediate safety-relevant layers (where separability grows fastest), projects over-refusal gradients onto the orthogonal complement of the rank-$k=10$ harmful refusal subspace, and applies a final-layer Circle Loss ($\lambda = 0.1$) with a class-balanced FIFO memory bank ($|\mathcal{M}| = 128$) to maintain deep-layer representation separation.

## Key Quantitative Findings

- **Over-Refusal Mitigation vs. Safety Trade-off**: Trained on 500 examples (`200` harmful, `200` benign, `100` over-refusal), GOAL achieves best or tied-best Compliance Rate (`C.R.`) in **13 of 15** main benchmark settings across `XSTest` (`225`), `OR-Bench-Hard` (`1,269`), `PHTest` (`2,052`), `OKTest` (`300`), and `CoCoNot` (`379`), with relative compliance gains up to **`14.03%`** and safety gains up to **`1.20%`** on `JailbreakBench` (`100`) and `STL` (`516`), while keeping general capability (`MMLU`, `OBQA`, `PIQA`, `ARC-C`) degradation within **`0.0071`** accuracy:
  - `Gemma-7B-it`: XSTest C.R. rises from `0.6977` (Base) and `0.8089` (SFT) to **`0.9200`**; OR-Bench-Hard from `0.6225` (Base) and `0.5808` (SFT) to **`0.7006`**; OKTest from `0.6400` to **`0.8733`**; JailbreakBench safety rises from `0.9000` (Base) and `0.8800` (ACTOR) to **`0.9700`** (`0.9884` STL).
  - `Llama-2-7B-Chat`: XSTest C.R. rises from `0.8000` (Base) and `0.8978` (SFT) to **`0.9511`**; OR-Bench-Hard from `0.2648` (Base) and `0.6604` (SFT) to **`0.7699`**; PHTest from `0.5502` to **`0.9498`**; JailbreakBench safety remains **`0.9600`** (`0.9864` STL).
  - `Llama-2-13B-Chat`: XSTest C.R. rises from `0.7511` to **`0.9289`**; OR-Bench-Hard from `0.2002` (Base) and `0.5902` (SFT) to **`0.7297`**; PHTest from `0.6501` to **`0.9649`**; JailbreakBench safety reaches **`0.9700`** (`0.9981` STL).
  - `Qwen3-8B` (5 runs): XSTest **`0.9867`**, OR-Bench-Hard **`0.8613`**, PHTest **`0.9693`**, OKTest **`0.9900`**, CoCoNot **`0.9578`**, JailbreakBench **`0.9800`**, STL **`0.9953`**.
- **Probe-Guided Layer Selection**: Updating only the probe-selected intermediate window (`12–22` on `Llama-2-7B-Chat`, ~1/3 of layers) achieves `0.9511` XSTest and `0.7699` OR-Bench-Hard, matching full 32-layer tuning (`0.9556` / `0.7699`) and far outperforming early layers `1–11` (`0.8267` / `0.6194`), late layers `23–32` (`0.8089` / `0.5595`), or single-layer tuning at layer 13 (`0.7867` / `0.5343`).
- **Why Naive Fixes Fail**: Simply downweighting harmful gradients ($\lambda_{\text{harm}} = 0.50$) causes `JailbreakBench` safety to drop from `0.96` to `0.86` (`STL` from `0.9787` to `0.8779`). Orthogonalizing against benign gradients (`Benign Ortho`) drops OR-Bench-Hard compliance to `0.5122` because it strips task-helpfulness directions.
- **Human Quality Audit & Overhead**: On 200 audited responses (`Llama-2-7B-Chat`, 3 human raters, $\kappa = 0.71$), GOAL achieves **`76.0%` human / `78.5%` `GPT-5.1` Useful Compliance** (vs `70.0%` / `72.5%` for ACTOR and `66.5%` / `68.5%` for SFT), with only `+0.21%` online training time overhead (`9.55 min` vs `9.53 min` on an A800 GPU, plus `2.17 min` one-time SVD extraction).

## Why Not a Skill?

GOAL is a white-box LoRA fine-tuning algorithm that operates on internal transformer layer activations and per-sample parameter gradients during supervised model training. It does not define an inference-time agent workflow, tool-use protocol, or harness-level procedure that an agent can execute at runtime across software projects. Its actionable lessons for safety alignment and model-tuning pipelines are captured as a rule contribution in `output/rules/adk-security-and-evaluation.md`.

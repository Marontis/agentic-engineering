# Reconciling Privacy with Provenance for Language Model Watermarks

> **Source**: [arXiv:2611.06100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06100) | `src:2611-06100v1`
> **Authors**: Elise L. Han, Tatsunori Hashimoto (Stanford University)
> **Date**: November 2026

## Executive Summary

Standard language model (LM) watermarks enable third parties holding a detection key to classify whether *individual* documents were generated by a specific model. While useful for provenance, per-document detectability creates a fundamental privacy hazard: leaking or sharing the verification key allows an adversary to link sensitive anonymous texts (such as whistleblowing tips, medical inquiries, or peer reviews) back to users of a specific LM provider. Conversely, cryptographic zero-knowledge watermarks protect privacy by restricting detection to the secret-key holder, which prevents independent third-party auditors from verifying model usage at scale.

Han and Hashimoto resolve this tension by reframing watermark verification from **individual document attribution** to **population-level prevalence estimation** under **$\varepsilon$-local differential privacy ($\varepsilon$-LDP)**. Their **Red-Green Sequence (RGS)** watermark perturbs the sequence-level distribution of generated texts rather than biasing token-by-token logits: a keyed 128-bit `BLAKE2b` hash partitions complete candidate responses into "Green" (with baseline probability $\pi_0$) and "Red" sets, and an $\varepsilon$-LDP rejection sampler accepts Green responses with probability $1$ and Red responses with probability $e^{-\varepsilon}$. Under this guarantee, any post-processing test on a single document is information-theoretically bounded near random guessing ($\mathrm{TPR} \le e^\varepsilon \mathrm{FPR}$), yet an auditor given a corpus of $n$ documents can accurately estimate the fraction $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ of watermarked text via a debiased linear plugin estimator—requiring fewer than `1.5` base model samples per response at $\varepsilon \in \{0.5, 1\}$ and `~2` samples at $\varepsilon \in \{2, 3\}$.

## Key Findings

1. **Sequence-Level $\varepsilon$-LDP Bounds Individual Attribution While Preserving Corpus Prevalence**:
   - A watermarking mechanism is $\varepsilon$-LDP if, for every prompt $x$, key $k$, and pair of candidate outputs $y, y'$, the watermarked generation distribution $Q_k$ relative to the base LM $P$ satisfies:
     $$\frac{Q_k(y \mid x)}{P(y \mid x)} \cdot \frac{P(y' \mid x)}{Q_k(y' \mid x)} \le e^\varepsilon$$
   - By operating at the **sequence level** rather than the token level, the privacy loss budget $\varepsilon$ does not degrade linearly with output token length $T$. For any binary detector applied to a single document, $\mathrm{TPR}(\mathcal{A}) \le e^\varepsilon \mathrm{FPR}(\mathcal{A})$ (e.g., at $\varepsilon = 1$ and $\mathrm{FPR} = 0.01$, individual detection power cannot exceed `0.027`).

2. **Red-Green Sequence (RGS) Rejection Sampling Is Computationally Cheap**:
   - Given a keyed hash $h_k : \mathcal{V}^* \to \{R, G\}$ with null green rate $\pi_0 = \Pr_{k}[h_k(y) = G]$ (implemented via 128-bit `BLAKE2b` with threshold $T = \lfloor \pi_0 2^{128} \rfloor$), Algorithm 2 draws $Y \sim P(\cdot \mid x)$ (truncated to prefix length $T$ tokens for hashing, then completed under $P$) and accepts $Y$ immediately if $h_k(Y) = G$, or with probability $e^{-\varepsilon}$ if $h_k(Y) = R$ (capped at $M$ candidates).
   - By Fact 1, the expected number of rejection sampling trials is bounded by $1 / (\Pr[h_k(Y)=G] + e^{-\varepsilon}\Pr[h_k(Y)=R])$. Empirically on C4 across `Qwen3-1.7B`, `Qwen3-4B`, `Qwen3-8B`, and `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`, RGS requires **`< 1.5` base samples per response** at $\varepsilon \in \{0.5, 1\}$ and **`~2` samples** at $\varepsilon \in \{2, 3\}$, with estimator accuracy plateauing by candidate budget $M = 8$.

3. **Debiased Prevalence Estimator Accurately Recovers Mixture Fractions**:
   - Under the high-entropy idealized randomized-response (RR) channel, a watermarked response hashes to Green with probability $\pi_1(\varepsilon, \pi_0) = \frac{\pi_0}{\pi_0 + e^{-\varepsilon}(1-\pi_0)} > \pi_0$.
   - Given $n$ observed texts $Y_1, \dots, Y_n$ containing an unknown fraction $\lambda$ of watermarked outputs, the plugin prevalence estimator is:
     $$\hat{\lambda}_{\mathrm{plugin}} = \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{\bar{S}_n - \pi_0}{\pi_1(\varepsilon, \pi_0) - \pi_0}\right), \quad \bar{S}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf{1}\{h_k(Y_i) = G\}$$
   - On 500 C4 prompts ($n = 2000$ documents across $\lambda \in \{0.1, \dots, 0.9\}$, $T = 50$, $M = 20$, $\pi_0 = 0.5$), RGS achieves **`0.0019` MSE** and **`0.0038` mean absolute bias** at $\varepsilon = 1$ (5th–95th percentile half-width `0.072`), **`0.0037` mean absolute bias** (half-width `0.040`) at $\varepsilon = 2$, and **`0.001` mean absolute bias** (half-width `0.031`) at $\varepsilon = 3$.

4. **Finite-Entropy Downward Bias Is Mitigated by Tuning $\pi_0$ Above the RR Variance Optimum**:
   - When the base LM distribution $P(\cdot \mid x)$ has low collision entropy (such as low-temperature decoding or deterministic code generation), repeated sampling collides on the same modal sequence, making the realized green rate $\mu_1(k) \le \pi_1(\varepsilon, \pi_0)$ (Proposition 4) and inducing a systematic downward bias $d^{-1}\mathbb{E}_k[B(k)] \le 0$ where $d = \pi_1 - \pi_0$.
   - Although the idealized RR asymptotic variance is minimized at $\pi_0^* = \sigma(-\varepsilon/2)$ (which equals `0.27` at $\varepsilon = 2$, Proposition 5), low-entropy regimes (`Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct` at temperature `0.2`, $T = 20$, $\varepsilon = 2$) exhibit persistent downward bias at $\pi_0 = 0.27$ as $n$ grows; setting a higher null green rate $\pi_0 \in \{0.4, 0.5\}$ reduces finite-entropy bias and yields lower Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) at larger sample sizes.

## Why Not a Skill?

This paper introduces a statistical decoding and corpus-level prevalence estimation mechanism for language model providers and regulatory auditors, rather than a subtask-level workflow executed by an autonomous agent. Its value to agent systems engineering lies in its formal privacy-provenance trade-off and parameter selection guidelines for telemetry and synthetic-data auditing, making a **Research Brief** the appropriate tier.

---

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **Replace per-document watermark detectors with sequence-level $\varepsilon$-LDP prevalence estimators when auditing sensitive corpora**: If an organization needs to measure what fraction of support tickets, code submissions, or training documents originate from an internal LLM without exposing individual authors to attribution risk, use sequence-level RGS with $\varepsilon \in [1, 2]$ and $M = 8$ rejection sampling cap (`< 1.5–2` base samples per response).
2. **Hash only a fixed non-thinking prefix ($T = 50\text{–}100$ tokens) and generate the remainder freely**: You do not need to resample entire multi-thousand-token completions during rejection sampling; applying the `BLAKE2b` green/red test to the first $T = 50\text{–}100$ visible output tokens (after stripping internal `<think>` blocks) suffices for sequence-level LDP and cuts rejection sampling cost to $T$ tokens per rejected trial.
3. **Set $\pi_0 \in [0.4, 0.5]$ rather than the theoretical variance-optimal $\sigma(-\varepsilon/2)$ when outputs have low entropy**: For code generation, structured extraction, or low-temperature agents (`temperature <= 0.2`), collision on modal prefixes causes downward prevalence bias; raising $\pi_0$ to `0.4–0.5` sacrifices minimal small-sample variance to prevent systematic underestimation at scale.

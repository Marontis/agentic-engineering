# Indirect Tipping: A Social Attack Surface in AI Agent Populations

**Source**: Flint et al., "Indirect tipping: a social attack surface in
AI agent populations" (arXiv:2609.25194), Sep 2026.

## Key Findings

- Standard critical-mass framework underestimates system vulnerability
  by reducing the problem to singular tipping points and ignoring
  indirect routes
- Indirect tipping through intermediate stepping-stone equilibria can
  REDUCE the committed minority required to reach an alternative state
- Stepping-stones can bypass majority requirements and make possible
  transitions inaccessible through direct challenges
- The diversity of available alternatives and timing of attack reshape
  the vulnerability landscape
- An equilibrium's resistance is NOT an intrinsic property but a
  structural feature of its competitive relations with alternative states

## Relevance to Agentic Engineering

Novel threat model for agent populations: adversaries can use indirect
pathways through stepping-stone equilibria rather than direct attacks.
This means security of a multi-agent coordination equilibrium depends
on the topology of ALL alternative states, not just the distance to
the nearest attacker-desired state.

## Why Not a Skill?

This is a theoretical and experimental characterization of a threat
model. The contribution is a new way to think about multi-agent
population security, not a procedural defense. The insight (map the
social landscape of coordination equilibria) is too abstract for a
step-by-step skill.

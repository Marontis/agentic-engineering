## Why Not a Skill?

Per `output/AGENTS.md` offensive-tooling policy, this paper trains an adversarial reinforcement-learning generator (`Symbolic-Swapper`, fine-tuned with SFT + GRPO on `Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct`) to synthesize deceptive action, predicate, object, and attribute names that break LLM planners. We omit adversarial prompt templates and attack-policy training recipes, and capture the paper strictly as a **defensive Research Brief** documenting why frontier reasoning planners and LLM-to-solver frameworks collapse by **over `70%`** (up to **`96.9%` relative**) under semantics-preserving symbol renaming, why models exhibit a sharp **domain-comprehension vs. multi-step planning gap**, and how to harden agentic planners and evaluation benchmarks in [`../rules/agent-evaluation-quality.md`](../rules/agent-evaluation-quality.md).

## Core Defensive Findings

### 1. Threat Model & Evaluation Setup: Solver-Verified Semantics-Preserving Symbol Mapping

Classical symbolic and SMT planners (`Fast Downward`, `Z3`) are strictly invariant to identifier names: renaming `pick_up(x)` and `holding(x)` to arbitrary single-token strings leaves transition preconditions, effects, initial state $s_0$, and goal condition $g$ logically identical. In contrast, deployed LLM agents routinely encounter non-commonsense identifiers in **enterprise API catalogs with autogenerated IDs, learned robotic/world-model operators, formal verification lemmas, IT workflow UUIDs, or poisoned middleware/OpenAPI schemas** where an attacker renames identifiers without altering runtime execution semantics.

Obadinma and Zhu evaluate **`250` solver-verified instances per condition** (`50` obfuscated domains $\times$ `5` instances, rejecting duplicates and confirming exact solvability with a symbolic verifier before testing) across **three discrete tasks**:
- **`Minigrid Go To Object` (`GTO`)**: Fully observable `4 x 4` grid (`4` actions, `9` predicates, `2` objects from `{key, ball, box}`, `6` color descriptors)—an intentionally easy short-horizon baseline.
- **`Minigrid Unlock`**: Two `4 x 4` rooms separated by a locked door (`5` actions, `12` predicates, `door` + `key`), requiring ordered subgoals (`find/pick_up key -> navigate -> toggle door`).
- **`PlanBench Blocks World` (`BW`)**: Classical PDDL block stacking (`4` actions, `5` predicates, up to `5` blocks `a..e`).

Four planning scaffolds are tested across `None` (canonical names), `Random-Swapper` (`R-Sw`, NLTK English words), `Base-Swapper` (`B-Sw`, unoptimized `Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct`), and `Symbolic-Swapper` (`Sy-Sw`, SFT + GRPO + `text-embedding-3-small` semantic-distance reward + `GPT-4.1-mini` trace-feedback memory scored against `Deepseek-V3.2 Reasoner`).

Lexical priors corrupt the auto-formalization *before* the solver executes.

### 3. Mechanistic Diagnosis: Self-Correction Suppression & The Perception-vs-Planning Gap

1. **Counterintuitive vocabularies suppress self-correction rather than causing perplexity collapse (`Tables 2 & 4`, `Appendix G`)**:
   - On `Deepseek-R1` `ZS` `GTO`, canonical domains elicit **`61.1` self-correction tokens** (`#SCT`, e.g., `wait`, `actually`, `let me reconsider`; `13` regex patterns) and a **`0.03`** reasoning-failure rate (`RF`, `GPT-4.1-mini` monitor). On successful `Sy-Sw` domains (`>= 4/5` failed instances), `#SCT` drops **`15.7x` to `3.9`** (`19.1` overall; on `BW`, `#SCT` drops from `68.2` on failed attacks to `42.9` on successful attacks), average plan trace length *decreases* on `GTO` (`13,888.7 -> 11,629.4`), and `RF` jumps to **`0.80`** (`0.14 -> 0.36` on `BW`). Qualitative traces (`Figure 9`) show the model expressing uncertainty about symbol roles (*"Perhaps the objects are not what I think"*) and prematurely boxing an invalid plan rather than backtracking.
   - Word-level statistics (`Table 4`, `GTO`) confirm failures are **not** artifacts of high perplexity or token-length overflow: successful `Sy-Sw` vocabularies have *lower* perplexity (`15.99` vs. `17.47`), *shorter* symbol lengths (`11.29` vs. `15.75` chars), and *smaller* Levenshtein distance (`10.84` vs. `14.77`) than failed `Sy-Sw` vocabularies (`Original`: `9.20` PPL, `6.04` chars).
2. **Late-layer hidden-state divergence (`Figures 4 & 7`)**: Layer-by-layer hidden-state PCA on `Deepseek-R1` (`GTO` and `BW`) shows relatively stable divergence through middle transformer layers, followed by **sharp instability and high trajectory curvature in the final output layers** where action commitments form.
3. **The Perception-vs-Planning Gap — Pre-Planning Canonical Translation Recovers Performance (`Section 6.2`, `Tables 5–6`)**:
   - When `Deepseek-R1` and `o4-mini` are prompted in a **dedicated pre-planning pass** to infer the functional commonsense meaning of each obfuscated symbol from the transition schema *before* generating a plan, models recover symbol functionality accurately regardless of whether the vocabulary defeated direct planning (`Figure 3`):
     - On `o4-mini` `GTO` (`Table 5`), functional symbol similarity rises from **`0.09` to `0.82`** and plan success rebounds from **`0.60` to `0.98`**.
     - On `Blocks World` (`Table 6`), pre-translation lifts `o4-mini` similarity from `0.34 -> 0.49` and plan success from **`0.40 -> 0.64`**, and lifts `Deepseek-R1` similarity from `0.30 -> 0.53` and plan success from **`0.33 -> 0.55`**.
   - **Key diagnostic insight**: Frontier reasoning models possess sufficient structural comprehension to decode arbitrary transition schemas in isolation, **but fail when forced to perform lexical symbol decoding and multi-step state-space search simultaneously in a single reasoning pass**.

## Actionable Defensive Takeaways for Praxis

1. **Benchmark agentic and neuro-symbolic planners with solver-verified symbol-renaming suites ([`../rules/agent-evaluation-quality.md`](../rules/agent-evaluation-quality.md))**: Standard `PlanBench`/`Minigrid` accuracy conflates genuine state-space search with pretraining lexical shortcuts. Pair canonical benchmark runs with randomized (`R-Sw`) and semantically inverted/distant identifier mappings (`1 - \mathrm{Sim}(c, \hat{c})`) verified solvable by an exact PDDL/SMT solver so lexical-prior fragility is measured explicitly before deploying planners on autogenerated APIs or untrusted schemas.
2. **Decouple symbol canonicalization from multi-step planning when consuming external or machine-generated schemas**: When an LLM planner must operate over unfamiliar APIs, UUIDs, learned operators, or third-party MCP/workflow definitions, insert an isolated **Stage-0 Schema Canonicalizer** that maps opaque or misleading action/predicate identifiers to verified, collision-free functional aliases grounded strictly in their formal `(parameters, preconditions, effects)` specification before invoking `ReACT` or tree search (`0.60 -> 0.98` on `GTO`, `+22` to `+24 pp` on `BW`).
3. **Compile formal solver inputs deterministically from structured schemas rather than natural-language auto-formalization (`LLMFP`)**: When structured API schemas, W3C Thing Descriptions, or PDDL/OpenAPI specs already exist, compile SMT/PDDL constraints deterministically in code and invoke the symbolic solver directly (compare [`../skills/intent-ltl-mcp-orchestration/SKILL.md`](../skills/intent-ltl-mcp-orchestration/SKILL.md) and [`nametrap-identifier-poisoning-coding-agents.md`](nametrap-identifier-poisoning-coding-agents.md)) rather than routing raw third-party identifier names through free-form LLM solver-code synthesis (`0.98 -> 0.03` under `Sy-Sw`).

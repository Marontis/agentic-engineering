# SoL-Pi: Recursively Scaling Auto-Research Loops for Efficient Agent Harness

> **Source**: Liu et al., arXiv:2609.20519, Sep 2026
> **Status**: Research Brief — integrated system, not standalone procedure

## Why Not a Skill?

SoL-Pi describes four surviving harness mechanisms from a recursive self-improvement process. While the improvements are concrete, they form an integrated system rather than a transferable procedure — each mechanism is coupled to the specific harness architecture (action execution, context compaction, observation handling, delegated reading).

## Core Concept

Take a recursive self-improvement (RSI) approach at the **harness layer** — scale auto-research loops across increasingly numerous and diverse environments to yield reusable improvements that transfer beyond their development setting. Four mechanisms survived selection and form SoL-Pi: action execution optimization, context compaction, observation handling, and delegated reading.

## Key Findings

- **44.7–49.0% reduction in recorded token traffic** compared to native harnesses
- **~33% reduction in API cost** (estimated $8.75–$13.50/hr savings vs. native Codex/Claude Code)
- Performance comparable to Pi across GPT-5.6 Sol and Opus 5 on 51-task EdgeBench
- Key insight: harness-layer RSI produces transferable improvements, not just benchmark-specific gains

## Relevance to Praxis

- Validates that harness-level optimizations compound across diverse task types
- The four mechanism categories (action, context, observation, reading) provide a useful taxonomy for evaluating harness improvements
- Cost savings data provides decision criteria for harness investment ROI

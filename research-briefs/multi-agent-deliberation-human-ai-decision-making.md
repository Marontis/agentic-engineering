# Understanding the Effects of Multi-Agent Deliberation for Human-AI Decision-Making

> **Source**: [arXiv:2611.14901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14901) — Harry Yizhou Tian, Ming Yin (2026)
> **Praxis Source ID**: `src:2611-14901v1`
> **License**: [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) | **Repository**: [Marontis/agentic-engineering](https://github.com/Marontis/agentic-engineering)

## Why Not a Skill?

This paper is an empirical two-study human-subjects investigation (`N = 401` and `N = 417` passed participants on Prolific across `8` randomized between-subjects treatments per study) evaluating when Multi-Agent Deliberation (`MAD`) improves or harms human-AI decision accuracy and subjective user experience relative to single-AI multi-round chat (`Chatbot`), single-AI single-round interaction (`Chatbot-R1`), and static two-AI panels (`Static`). It contributes empirical human-factors mechanisms, boundary conditions, and persona/engagement design tradeoffs rather than a reusable algorithmic subtask procedure, making it best captured as a research brief paired with decision rules in [`agent-human-interaction.md`](../rules/agent-human-interaction.md) and [`multi-agent-coordination.md`](../rules/multi-agent-coordination.md).

## Experimental Setup (`Section 3`, `Appendices C–D`)

Two randomized, between-subjects Prolific studies (`US` adults, $\ge 98\%$ approval, $\ge 100$ prior submissions) tested human-AI verification across `8` conditions (`Chatbot`, `Static`, plus a $3 \times 2$ factorial of AI Persona [`Base`, `Expert`, `DA` Devil's Advocate] $\times$ Human Engagement [`MAD-Active`, `MAD-Passive`]):

- **Tasks & Participants**:
  - **Study 1 — `ReaLMistake` (LLM Response Verification)**: Filtered to `125` math/logic-free verification items (`37` valid, `88` invalid) where humans could meaningfully inspect claims and evidence without specialized expertise; `6` items per participant (`421` recruited, **`N = 401`** passed attention checks, `~50` per treatment; `$3.00` base + `$0.20` bonus per correct final answer).
  - **Study 2 — `Fact-check` (Post-Cutoff Political Claim Verification)**: `48` balanced US political claims from `PolitiFact` and `Snopes` spanning January 2025–April 2026 (`12` each of `Democrat-True`, `Democrat-False`, `Republican-True`, `Republican-False`), grounded at runtime via `Tavily` web search (top-3 results per query across two queries, merged to at most `6` snippets excluding fact-checking domains); `8` items per participant (`2` per group; `439` recruited, **`N = 417`** passed attention checks, `~52` per treatment; `$2.00` base + `$0.10` bonus per correct final answer). Partisan breakdown (`Appendix B.4`): `Democrat N = 181`, `Republican N = 166`, `Other N = 70`.
- **LLM Backbone & Protocol**: Cross-family pair of `GPT-5.4` (`gpt-5.4-2026-03-05`) and `Claude Sonnet 4.5` (`claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`) at `temperature = 1.0, top_p = 0.9`, `200`-word response cap, over `3` rounds. Participants always recorded an independent pre-AI judgment first; in `MAD-Active`, the human spoke first in each round followed by the two AIs in randomized order; in `MAD-Passive`, the user reviewed the completed `3`-round two-AI transcript before deciding.
- **Persona Implementations (`Appendix C.2`)**:
  - `Base`: No persona prompt; deliberative guidelines instructing agents to engage substantively and not flip solely due to peer disagreement.
  - `Expert`: Task-tailored complementary personas generated by `Claude Opus 4.7` (`t = 0`) — `Fact-checker` + `Analyst` on `ReaLMistake`, and `Investigative Journalist` + `Policy & Governance Analyst` on `Fact-check`.
  - `DA` (Devil's Advocate): One randomly selected AI received an every-round prompt instruction to argue *against* the other AI's Round-1 answer and maintain the opposing stance across all `3` rounds without revealing the persona.

## Core Quantitative Findings (`Section 4`, `Appendices B.1–B.4`)

### 1. `RQ1`: `MAD` vs. Single-AI and Static Two-AI Baselines (`Section 4.1`, Holm-Bonferroni $t$-tests)

| Task | `MAD-Active-Base` Final Human Acc. | `Chatbot-R1` (1-Rd, 1-AI) | `Chatbot` (3-Rd, 1-AI) | `Static` (1-Rd, 2 Independent AIs) | Key Takeaway |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **`ReaLMistake`** | **`0.733 ± 0.203`** | `0.605 ± 0.200` ($p = 0.007$) | `0.639 ± 0.205` ($p = 0.027$) | `0.620 ± 0.223` ($p = 0.019$) | `MAD` beats all three baselines when single-AI accuracy leaves headroom. |
| **`Fact-check`** | **`0.948 ± 0.075`** | `0.849 ± 0.170` ($p < 0.001$) | Not sig. diff. (1-AI acc. `0.984 ± 0.042`) | `0.748 ± 0.228` ($p < 0.001$) | Multi-round interaction drives gains; 2nd AI adds nothing near single-AI ceiling (`Chatbot-R1` AI acc. `0.952 ± 0.076`). |

- **Where Gains Come From (`Section 4.1.2`, `Appendix B.3.1`)**:
  - Multi-round deliberation raised the **all-AI correct rate** over `Static` on both tasks ($p < 0.001$) and over both `Chatbot-R1` ($p < 0.001$) and `Chatbot` ($p = 0.006$) on `ReaLMistake`.
  - When all AIs were correct, `MAD-Active-Base` raised human adoption of correct answers over `Static` (`ReaLMistake` $p = 0.016$; `Fact-check` $p < 0.001$) and `Chatbot-R1` (`Fact-check` $p < 0.001$), matching `Chatbot`.
  - **Round-1 Dominance**: Most human accuracy gain occurred in Round 1 rather than Rounds 2–3 (`ReaLMistake`: `+0.156 ± 0.193` from initial to Round 1 vs. `+0.021 ± 0.101` from Round 1 to final, $p < 0.001$; `Fact-check`: `+0.227 ± 0.213` vs. `+0.057 ± 0.130`, $p < 0.001$), and full human-plus-AI consensus frequently formed before Round 3 (`Appendix B.3.2`).
- **Where `MAD` Fails — Shared AI Errors & Disagreements (`Sections 4.1.3–4.1.4`, `Appendix B.2.3`)**:
  - **Echo-chamber persistence with a human in the loop**: `MAD-Active-Base` did *not* reduce shared AI errors or improve human accuracy when both AIs were wrong on `ReaLMistake` (on the `16` correlated-error items [`12.8%`] where both LLMs erred $>50\%$ in `Static`, logistic regression estimated user accuracy at $\hat{p} = 0.149, \text{CI}=[0.031, 0.49]$ for `MAD-Active-Base` vs. $\hat{p} = 0.157, \text{CI}=[0.038, 0.466]$ for `Static`). Worse, on `Fact-check`, `Static` had a **`0.000 ± 0.000`** all-AI error rate whereas `MAD-Active-Base` *induced* shared AI errors ($p = 0.04$).
  - **Near-total consensus collapse & unimproved disagreement resolution**: By Round 3, AI disagreement in `MAD-Active-Base` collapsed from `0.191 ± 0.185` (`Static`: `0.284 ± 0.176`, $p = 0.011$) to **`0.017 ± 0.051`** on `ReaLMistake`, and from `0.041 ± 0.059` (`Static`: `0.084 ± 0.071`, $p = 0.001$) to **`0.004 ± 0.021`** on `Fact-check`. On items with initial AI disagreement, AIs converged to the *correct* answer `0.745 ± 0.440` (`ReaLMistake`) and `0.611 ± 0.502` (`Fact-check`) of the time, leaving human accuracy statistically indistinguishable from `Static` (`0.634 ± 0.366` vs. `0.543 ± 0.394` on `ReaLMistake`; `0.676 ± 0.466` vs. `0.583 ± 0.475` on `Fact-check`).

### 2. `RQ2`: Persona Configuration (`Base`, `Expert`, `DA`) & Human Engagement (`MAD-Active` vs. `MAD-Passive`) (`Section 4.2`)

- **Standing Devil's Advocate (`DA`) Degrades Overall Human Accuracy**:
  - **`ReaLMistake`**: Significant persona main effect ($F(2, 292) = 14.959, p < 0.001$); `DA` (`0.577 ± 0.208`) scored significantly below both `Base` (`0.678 ± 0.191`, Tukey $p < 0.001$) and `Expert` (`0.721 ± 0.185`, $p < 0.001$).
  - **`Fact-check`**: Significant persona main effect ($F(2, 353) = 5.452, p = 0.005$); `DA` (`0.875 ± 0.145`) scored significantly below `Expert` (`0.929 ± 0.104`, $p = 0.007$). (`Expert` vs. `Base` showed no overall human accuracy gain on either task, and `Expert` lowered all-AI correct rate vs. `Base` on `ReaLMistake`, $p = 0.048$.)
  - **Mechanism of `DA` Harm (`Appendix B.2.1`)**: Even though the non-DA AI retained high final accuracy (`0.783` active / `0.650` passive on `ReaLMistake`; `0.960` / `0.942` on `Fact-check`), `DA` left high unresolved Round-3 disagreement (`0.587` / `0.650` on `ReaLMistake`; `0.287` / `0.415` on `Fact-check`; initial `Fact-check` `DA` disagreement was `~0.792` vs. `~0.029–0.031` for `Base`/`Expert` because `Claude` sometimes refused to argue against irrefutable search snippets). Persistent disagreement triggered severe **human underreliance on the correct non-DA AI**: `0.298` / `0.426` overall (`0.312` / `0.473` under final disagreement) on `ReaLMistake`, and `0.139` / `0.089` overall (`0.308` / `0.131` under final disagreement) on `Fact-check`.
  - **Narrow `DA` Upside on Shared Errors (`Section 4.2.3`, `Appendix B.2.3`)**: On `ReaLMistake`, `DA` lowered the all-AI error rate vs. `Base` ($p = 0.005$), resolved correlated errors more often ($p = 0.019$ vs. `Base`, $p = 0.028$ vs. `Expert`), and raised user accuracy when both AIs ended wrong ($p = 0.008$ vs. `Base`, $p = 0.031$ vs. `Expert`; correlated-error items $p < 0.001$) — because `100% / 100%` (`ReaLMistake`) and `94.4% / 100%` (`Fact-check`) of `DA` all-AI errors occurred when the non-DA AI visibly flipped from right to wrong, alerting the human reviewer.
- **Active Participation (`MAD-Active`) vs. Passive Transcript Review (`MAD-Passive`)**:
  - `MAD-Active` significantly improved overall human decision accuracy on `ReaLMistake` ($F(1, 292) = 12.849, p < 0.001$; **`0.698 ± 0.214`** vs. **`0.619 ± 0.186`**), raised all-AI correct rates across both tasks with small main effects (`ReaLMistake`: $F(1, 292) = 9.694, p = 0.002, \eta_p^2 = 0.032$; `Fact-check`: $F(1, 302) = 15.888, p < 0.001, \eta_p^2 = 0.05$, with large rescue interactions inside `DA`: $d = 0.801$ and $d = 0.839, p < 0.001$), improved user accuracy when AIs were right on `ReaLMistake` ($F(1, 268) = 4.32, p = 0.039$), and lowered all-AI error rate on `ReaLMistake` ($F(1, 292) = 5.924, p = 0.016$), while showing no overall human accuracy difference on `Fact-check`.

### 3. `RQ3`: Subjective UX, Cognitive Load & Partisan Bias (`Section 4.3`, `Table 1`, `Appendix B.4`)

- **`MAD-Active-Base` vs. `Static`**: Users rated `MAD-Active-Base` significantly higher in AI helpfulness (`ReaLMistake` $p = 0.008$; `Fact-check` $p = 0.009$) and disagreement comfort ($p < 0.001$; $p = 0.019$) and lower in disagreement confusion ($p = 0.024$; $p = 0.037$), but reported significantly higher **NASA-TLX mental demand** ($p < 0.001$) and **effort** ($p < 0.001$) on search-heavy `Fact-check`.
- **Persona UX Costs**: Across both tasks, `DA` vs. `Base` significantly increased perceived AI complexity (`ReaLMistake` $F(2,292) = 5.643, p = 0.004$, Tukey $p = 0.002$; `Fact-check` $F(2,302) = 5.409, p = 0.005$, Tukey $p = 0.033$) and confusion (`ReaLMistake` $F(2,292) = 8.362, p < 0.001$, Tukey $p < 0.001$; `Fact-check` $F(2,302) = 4.005, p = 0.019$, Tukey $p = 0.017$), lowered trust (`ReaLMistake` $F(2,292) = 5.055, p = 0.007$, Tukey $p = 0.007$; `Fact-check` $F(2,302) = 3.106, p = 0.046$, Tukey $p = 0.032$), and spiked frustration (`ReaLMistake` $F(2,292) = 9.094, p < 0.001$, Tukey $p < 0.001$). `Expert` also raised frustration ($p = 0.013$) and confusion ($p = 0.008$) on `ReaLMistake` and complexity ($p = 0.006$) on `Fact-check` relative to `Base`.
- **Engagement UX Tradeoff**: `MAD-Active` significantly increased user frustration over `MAD-Passive` on both tasks (`ReaLMistake` $F(1,292) = 7.374, p = 0.007$; `Fact-check` $F(1,302) = 8.939, p = 0.003$), while reducing consensus pressure ($F(1,292) = 5.386, p = 0.021$) and disagreement confusion ($F(1,292) = 8.03, p = 0.005$) on `ReaLMistake` and increasing perceived disagreement helpfulness ($F(1,302) = 4.322, p = 0.038$) on `Fact-check`.
- **Partisan Bias (`Appendix B.4`)**: Only `Static` exhibited statistically nonzero $\Delta$ accuracy bias between own-party and other-party claims ($p = 0.043$); no `MAD` condition or $3 \times 2$ factor showed significant partisan bias.

## Relevance to Praxis

- **Adaptive Human-AI Deliberation & Persona Guardrails ([`agent-human-interaction.md`](../rules/agent-human-interaction.md))**: Establishes that multi-round deliberation is the primary accuracy driver over static multi-AI panels, adding a second AI pays off only when single-AI multi-round accuracy is below ceiling, and prompt-only every-round devil's advocate personas hurt human accuracy and trust—motivating adaptive escalation (`Static` $\to$ `MAD-Passive-Base` $\to$ `MAD-Active-Base` with early stopping once consensus forms).
- **Human-in-the-Loop Echo Chambers ([`multi-agent-coordination.md`](../rules/multi-agent-coordination.md))**: Extends [multi-agent-debate-peer-influence-tracing](multi-agent-debate-peer-influence-tracing.md) (`arXiv:2610.35930`) to cross-family human-AI deliberation (`GPT-5.4` + `Claude Sonnet 4.5`), proving that putting a human in the loop does not prevent shared AI misconceptions and can even induce new shared errors over independent panels (`Fact-check` $p = 0.04$).

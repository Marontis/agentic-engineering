# Sandman: Building and Operating Sandboxes for Agentic Reinforcement Learning on Shared Kubernetes

> **Source**: [arXiv:2610.39711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39711) — Yiming Yan, Hongyang Wang, Wenbo Chen, Xuan Wang, Yichuan Li, Jingguo Liu, Xian Li, Leo Fan, Chen Qi, Siwei Li, Qianli Wu, Ruochen Wen, Qian Qu, Zheshen Wang, Benoit Dumoulin (Amazon), published 2026-10-29. Praxis ID: `src:2610-39711v1`.

## Summary

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) for software engineering (`SWE-bench Verified`, `SWE-Gym`) pairs bursty, stateful multi-turn rollout sandboxes with short, CPU-intensive grading sandboxes running untrusted model-generated code and tests. Yan et al. report four months of operational measurements, failure analyses (`41` documented incidents), and controlled experiments from **Sandman**, a Kubernetes-native sandbox service backing `122` production GRPO training runs (`21,407` NVIDIA B200-hours across 4 campaigns, `1,033,764` trajectory end events in Campaign D alone) on a shared cluster (`2,688` B200 GPUs across `336` nodes and `375` schedulable 96-vCPU nodes).

The paper makes six core engineering and measurement contributions:

1. **Workload Asymmetry**: Rollout sandboxes (`2 vCPU`, `8 GiB`) spend **`74.35%–91.38%`** of their lifetime **idle** waiting for LLM token generation (`1.7–3.0 s` median turn), using only **`0.43%–2.80%`** of their CPU request (`0.001–0.007 vCPU` while generating) and **`1.9%`** of their memory request, whereas grading pods keep **`0.70–0.89` cores** busy. Even with outbound network access blocked, **`46%–94%`** of rollouts attempt package installs or downloads, driving **`70%–98%`** of all commands $\ge 10\text{ s}$.
2. **Provider-Owned Lifecycle Cleanup Across 25 Cancellation/Error Windows**: When a caller times out and cancels an in-flight pod create, naive error handling leaks the accepted pod (leaking `5,904` 8-GiB pods in one `1,536`-concurrency stress test and wasting `974–1,347` B200-hours). Conversely, deleting by pod name on any create error deletes co-tenants' pods `100%` (`40/40`) of the time on HTTP `409 Conflict`. Shielding the create call, attaching a per-create nonce label, reading back on `5xx`/transport errors, and deleting with a UID precondition plus a `1–30 s` retry queue eliminated leaks across all **25 windows (`4,000` trials)** with `0/1,000` co-tenant deletions.
3. **Shared-Cluster Bottlenecks (Webhooks, Service Links, VPC CNI Warm IPs)**:
   - Unpaced `4,096`-trajectory bursts collapsed a single-replica admission webhook (`84.9%–85.4%` trajectory failure, `21,517` HTTP 500s). Scaling the webhook to 3 replicas (`1 GiB`) and pacing creates (`10–25/s`, deletes at `2x`) scaled cleanly to **`11,858` live sandboxes** with `0` admission errors in `38,377` creates.
   - Kubernetes's default `enableServiceLinks: true` injected `~23,400` environment variables from `3,255` namespace Services into every container, inflating median `bash` tool latency from `0.63 s` to `2.12–3.4 s`. Disabling Service links and routing directly by Pod IP reduced median `bash` call latency to **`16–22 ms`** (and no-op command latency to **`2.0 ms`** vs `14.3 ms` on `agent-sandbox` and `203.5 ms` on `OpenSandbox`).
   - Default bin-packing exhausted per-node VPC CNI warm IPs (`25.4%` address-assignment failures, create-to-Ready `p99 = 57 s`). A soft node topology spread (`maxSkew: 2`) eliminated address-assignment failures (**`0%`**) and cut create-to-Ready **p99 to `8 s`** (`7.1x` reduction).
4. **In-Container (`execd`) Hardening**: Running `bash` in its own process group with group kill + an active `2 s` command health probe cut trajectory timeouts from `32.6%` to `22.4%`; pre-parsing commands with `bash -n` turned `300 s` heredoc/quote hangs into `3–18 ms` refusals (`exit 2`); strict decimal status-frame parsing eliminated `100%` (`200/200`) of 4 KiB read-boundary false-zero exit statuses; capping output floods at `100 KiB` while draining kept `execd` memory at `21.5 MiB` (vs `3,106.2 MiB` / OOM kill); and byte-exact file reads with length + SHA-256 verification prevented 2,000-line display truncation (`655,315` lines lost across `408` files) and CRLF/encoding corruption (`892/892` files).
5. **Grading Condition Sensitivity & Vacuous Official Tests**:
   - A $2^{8-4}$ fractional factorial study (`22,000` gradings across `500` `SWE-bench Verified` tasks and `16,807` gradings across `2,401` `SWE-Gym` tasks) showed that **`3.0%` (`15/500`)** of Verified reference patches (`11` due to denied egress, `4` due to 1-core affinity/CPU quota) and **`4.79%` (`115/2,401`)** of SWE-Gym tasks flip verdicts across run conditions. Re-grading SWE-Gym training patches with open egress flipped `1.35%` (natural) and `4.9%` (enriched) of training labels from `0` to `1`.
   - Two `SWE-bench Verified` tasks (`django-7530`, `django-10097`) omit fix-checking tests so **empty patches pass**; **`22.9%` (`1,233 / 5,377`)** of Campaign D's positive rewards on those tasks (`605 / 1,148` learned GRPO groups) rewarded non-fixes. A pre-job **Reference-Pass / Empty-Fail Check** (`1.49%` of grading pod-hours) plus an **In-Pod Run-Condition Probe** (`240/240` mismatches caught) and **Content-Fingerprinted Baseline State Check** (`0 / 787` poisoned reuse errors vs `64 / 787` for plain `git reset/clean`) close these reward leaks.
6. **Critical-Path Accounting & Pre-Registered Training Trade-offs**:
   - Rollout pod creation sits on the learner's critical path in only `0.0%–0.9%` of step time; removing all sandbox waiting is estimated via Little's law to save **`~8.5%`** of run time (`~1,830` of `21,095` B200-hours).
   - Warm pools cut median grading acquisition by `97%–99.5%` (`5.2–7.5 s` $\to$ `26–247 ms`) and time to step 24 by `9.4%`, but cost **`2.46x` sandbox vCPU-hours**; on-demand pod creation uses the fewest pod-hours across all 8 replayed traces.
   - In a 6-pair pre-registered GRPO comparison (`150` steps on `Qwen3.5-35B-A3B`), dropping the 3 uncertified tasks instead of repairing their tests and capping the reuse pool at `32` pods (`0.3 creates/s`) scored **`0.4611` vs `0.4844` (`-2.33` points)** on `112` held-out tasks—showing that task certification should trigger **test-suite repair or run-condition adjustment** rather than silent task deletion, and pool caps must not bottleneck burst grading.

## Why Not a Skill?

While Sandman contains highly actionable engineering patterns for container sandboxes and SWE benchmark graders, the paper is fundamentally a multi-month production post-mortem and empirical measurement study tied to a shared Kubernetes + Ray + GRPO stack, including nuanced negative findings on warm-pool cost (`2.46x` vCPU-hours) and task-dropping (`-2.33` held-out points). Its concrete failure-prevention invariants are distilled into always-active rules in `output/rules/agent-sandbox-safety.md` and `output/rules/agent-evaluation-quality.md`.
